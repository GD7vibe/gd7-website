    // 06 INSIGHTS

    Challenging how the industry works

    Two positions from GD7 that question accepted practice in offshore wind.

    UXO clearance as it stands is not needed, and it needs reform

    Cable route UXO clearance is often driven by a blanket default: assume a hazard, clear everything, price in the programme risk. GD7's position is that a properly evidenced, risk-based assessment should decide what actually has to be removed, how much residual risk is acceptable, and what can be avoided or left in place.

    • Replace blanket clearance with quantified risk assessment
    • Use route engineering and avoidance before disposal
    • Cut programme, cost and environmental impact

    Tunnel the connection between offshore and onshore substations

    Cable landfalls are a recurring consenting, programme and environmental pain point. New tunnelling technology is lowering cost and increasing advance rates, which makes a bored connection between the onshore and offshore sides a serious option for new wind farms.

    • Less disruption at beaches, dunes and the intertidal zone
    • Fewer weather-window constraints than open-sea cable laying
    • Protected, accessible and replaceable cable route
    • Rapidly improving tunnelling cost and speed
